“−2” is the whole point of this module

Credit Risk has raised eight requisitions against a plan of six. Nobody approved that as an exception — it happened one requisition at a time, and every individual approval was reasonable. Only a screen that holds the year in view can see it, which is why the row is coloured rather than left as a number the reader has to subtract themselves.

Every number in the “Raised” column depends on a link that does not exist yet

Counting requisitions against a plan needs Requisition → HeadcountPlan, which the module doc lists as an open question and asks to be settled early. Without it these columns can only be typed in by hand, and a hand-typed “Raised” is wrong within a week. Decide the relationship before building this table.

Agency fees, in an in-house product

It looks like a leftover from the agency era and it is not — it is in the module's own category list. A company that runs its own talent team still uses an agency for the occasional hard role, and the money is exactly what a budget screen is for. Reading it beside the cost of doing it in-house is the argument the HR Director takes to the CFO.

Where does a spend row come from?

Unresolved in the module doc: typed in by a recruiter, or pulled from a finance system. It changes this screen completely. Typed in, it needs an entry form, an approval and an audit trail, and it will drift from the general ledger. Pulled, it needs a finance integration nobody has scoped, and this screen becomes read-only. Drawn read-only, which is the honest state until somebody decides — a data-entry form would be the more expensive guess.

What this screen cannot decide

When does the year start?

Myanmar's public-sector fiscal year has moved more than once, and a bank may not use the calendar year either. “FY 2027” in the header is a label over an assumption. If the start month is configurable, every month bucket, every “month 9 of 12” and every year-on-year comparison in Module 5 depends on that setting.

One currency, or more?

Everything here is MMK. A bank hiring an expatriate specialist, or paying an overseas job board in USD, breaks that quietly — totals become meaningless without a rate and a date. Cheap to decide now, expensive after the first plan is approved.

Can a plan be re-forecast mid-year?

Credit Risk is already two over. The realistic response is a revision, and a revision of an approved number has to go back through the chain — otherwise “approved plan” means the version nobody is working to. Module 1's revise-and-resubmit rounds are the obvious shape, and are not drawn here because the question is open.

Does the plan block a requisition?

The eighth Credit Risk requisition was approved against a plan of six. Should the seventh have been refused, warned about, or routed to an extra approver? All three are defensible; only the first is a product that says no to its own users, and that has to be an explicit choice rather than a side effect of adding a column.
